Mission-phase risks

Day 29 of flight

NASA's five hazards of long missions, with NASA's numbers. The day 29 lines are our reading of NASA's timings.

Space radiation

"Invisible to the human eye, space radiation is not only stealthy but considered one of the most hazardous aspects of spaceflight."

Source: NASA Human Research Program, 5 Hazards of Human Spaceflight

Isolation and confinement

"Behavioral responses occur among groups of people far from Earth who are isolated and confined in a small space over a long period of time."

Source: NASA Human Research Program, Hazard: Isolation and Confinement

Distance from Earth

"Instructions, new supplies, medical care, and more become increasingly challenging to receive from Earth as astronauts venture deeper into space."

Source: NASA Human Research Program, Hazard: Distance From Earth

Gravity fields

"Floating weightlessly within a spacecraft can lead to dizziness, bone and muscle loss, vision issues, and more."

Source: NASA Human Research Program, Hazard: Gravity Fields

Space adaptation syndromeDay 29: usually past

"Space Adaptation Syndrome (SAS) occurs in up to 70% of first-time fliers (30% of whom may experience vomiting) during the first 48 to 72 hours of microgravity."

Source: NASA-STD-3001 Vol 2 Rev C, [V2 7102] rationale, p.96

Headward fluid shiftDay 29: ongoing

The total fluid shift was reported to be 2 L, most of it in the first 6 to 10 hours after entering microgravity.

Source: NTRS 20180000936, Evidence Report: Risk of Spaceflight Associated Neuro-ocular Syndrome (SANS), pp.69–70

Source for "ongoing": NTRS 20140003729, Evidence Report: Risk of Decompression Sickness (DCS), p.11

Bone lossDay 29: ongoing

"Without the continuous load of Earth’s gravity, weight-bearing bones lose on average 1% to 1.5% of mineral density per month during spaceflight."

Source: NASA Human Research Program, Hazard: Gravity Fields

With resistive exercise on ARED, hip and spine loss has been reduced to 0.3 to 0.5% per month.

Source: NASA Evidence Report: Risk of Early Onset Osteoporosis Due to Space Flight, p.21

Hostile closed environments

"Maintaining a safe ecosystem inside a vehicle presents unique challenges, from ensuring optimal temperatures, pressures, and lighting, to reducing noise, monitoring microbial communities, and tracking immune responses."

Source: NASA Human Research Program, Hazard: Hostile/Closed Environments
