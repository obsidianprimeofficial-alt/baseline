# Evidence

One row per rule. The card text and sources are in `docs/evidence_map.json` (copied to `web/data/evidence_map.json`); this table says what each source is and how strong it is. Reviewed 24 Oct 2026.

Every signal rule compares today with the mean and spread (SD) of the same person's previous days (up to 30, at least 14; today never counts). A day 2 SD out in the direction that matters is a watch item; 3 SD, or 2 SD two days running, is a flag.

NASA-STD-3001 Vol 1 Rev C [V1 3004] = NASA Space Flight Human-System Standard Volume 1: Crew Health (2023), requirement V1 3004, https://standards.nasa.gov/sites/default/files/standards/NASA/C//nasa-std-3001-vol-1-rev-c-signature.pdf. It requires vital signs to be monitored and trended, and private two-way contact with ground medical support. It backs the "report to the flight surgeon" part of a step, not any measurement advice.

## Review notes (24 Oct 2026)

- **NTRS 20140012988** (Clark 2014, conference abstract JSC-CN-30697): says the new ISS lighting assembly will be used to control the crew's circadian rhythm; no sleep or performance data. Kept on the reaction step for the lighting-schedule words only.
- **NTRS 20140002864** (Ploutz-Snyder 2013, conference abstract JSC-CN-29760): names T2, ARED and CEVIS and says crew results on standard medical tests improved with them; no HRV data. Kept on the HRV step; removed from the 48-hour step.
- **OSD-140** (GLDS-140, DOI 10.26030/pybz-6s43; Hou et al. 2022, iScience, DOI 10.1016/j.isci.2022.104493): ground study, 6 male crew, 36 blood samples at 6 time points; describes isolation-related mood state and biochemical changes alongside epigenetic changes. Both card texts kept as written. Checked on osdr.nasa.gov 24 Oct.
- Core-temperature step reworded: no NASA source was found for lowering exercise load or for fluid intake, so the step only repeats the reading and reports it.
